# Religious Misattribution
by Sven Nilsen, 2026

I grew up in a small fundamentalist Christian church in Norway.

Fundamentalist Christianity, the way I understand it, is something that is only possible in general,
when a society is transitioning from a low literacy, low information environment to high literacy, high information environment.

These two conditions must be met, because it is not possible for a low literacy society to misattribute new ideas that belong to a high literacy society.
Likewise, any person who comes from a low information society understands that new ideas in a high information society does not originate in their own society.

Today, I am an atheist. My worldview has changed.

Now, this blog post is not about me. I am not trying to argue here for my own worldview.

The topic I want to bring up in this blog post, is religious misattribution.

Religious misattribution is one of the primary mechanisms behind fundamentalist Christianity,
so I thought it would be interesting to go more into depth about this concept.

Misattribution is simply when a person thinks one idea comes from another source than where it came from.

If you strive to avoid misattribution in general, then religious misattribution is easier to avoid.
It follows that it gets harder to be a fundamentalist Christian, because as you attribute ideas correctly,
your own worldview can be explained, even when your own worldview remained unchanged,
not in terms of one religion only, but a set of religions,
where ideas pollinated intellectually between practitioners of these religions.

Fundamentalist Christianity is not just a worldview. It is also a feeling, an emotion, that there is a "pure" religion in some sense.

Basically, in Christianity, this feeling of a "pure" religion, is that there is one savior figure, Jesus Christ,
which supposedly had the "pure" religion pinned down, while he lived.

Now, if you believe Jesus Christ was God in human form, then this feeling that Jesus Christ had the "pure" religion pinned down, might seem justified.

The problem is that when your worldview is shaped by an idea, and that idea came from somewhere else than Jesus Christ,
there is no need to believe that Jesus Christ had the "pure" religion pinned down,
because you do not need Jesus Christ as a savior figure to explain all the things you believe.
Instead, at the very minimum, there are some things you believe, that Jesus Christ most probably never even thought about,
unless you give Jesus Christ some kind of super-natural power that most people do not have.

Fundamentalist Christianity, under correct attribution of ideas, hinges on the belief that Jesus Christ has some super-natural power.
This super-natural power is basically, despite no evidence that Jesus Christ ever thought about what you believe,
you still believe Jesus Christ could have thought about what you believe, because he is God.

Do you see how this works?

If you attribute ideas correctly, in a rigorous academic way, then it gets hard to be a fundamentalist Christian,
because as you study, it becomes apparent that what you believe, even your own worldview has not changed while studying,
does not come from something Jesus Christ said, nor from Jesus' disciples, nor even from apostles in general.

Instead, as you study, it becomes more and more apparent that what you believe is based on a set of religions, not a single religion.

This fact comes from simply attributing ideas correctly.
The feeling of a "pure" religion, is based on beliefs in a worldview, that is in fact not a "pure" religion.
Somehow, that particular worldview gave you this feeling, but there is no evidence supporting it, just a feeling.

In principle, a person can remain a fundamentalist Christian, while also embracing that their worldview came from a set of religions.
Now, one reason most people stop being fundamentalist Christians after doing a lot of study,
is because this feeling of a "pure" religion was so important, that when they no longer believe there is any evidence to support it,
they also start questioning why they are doing this to themselves.
As soon as you stop believing in a "pure" religion in this sense, the whole house of cards comes down.
Once you start questioning your own worldview, you will figure that you do not actually have good reasons to believe what you believe.

### Growing up as a fundamentalist Christian is like being told you have only one parent

Imagine a child that is raised by its father,
but the father tells the child that he is the only parent.
There was no "mother" involved.

As soon as the child learns about how children are made, it will start questioning whether the father tells the truth.

Now, one thing is to learn that your father has lied to you, this entire time.

You might still love your own father. However, this love was also based on the idea that you could trust your father.
When you figure out that this trust was not earned, but given naively through the ignorance of a child,
it is very likely that you get problems in the relationship with your father.
This relationship might start feeling strained and exhausting.

However, there is a completely different thing to learn that you might actually have a mother.

Who was she? Is she still alive? Where did she live? What did she do? How does she look like?

Over time, these questions start to dominate how the child is thinking about the situation.
The father is just a douche-bag, right, who want your love all for himself. Ignore him.

The problems that start to appear in the relationship between the child and its father,
soon gets put to the side by the sheer curiosity the child feels, about what it does not know yet.

### There must be at least two religions in the set and you have one. Where is the other?

The feeling that what you have is a "pure" religion goes away
and gets replaced by another feeling, that what you believe is part something you can trace back in time,
but there is also a lot of things you do not know yet.

One thing you know, is that once you find this other religion, you might recognize it,
because you already know what you believe.
It is kind of like looking in a book, searching for a mirror of yourself.

A problem is that the religions that exist today, also came from their own sets of religions.
Figuring out how your religion came from a set of religion, is related to the more general problem of figuring
out how any religion came from a set of religions in its past.

Another problem is that even when you turn back the clock, the religions in the past also came from their own sets of religions further back in time.

It is not hard to figure out that this process might get a lot messier than you thought.

There are two symmetries here:

1. Space symmetry: Figuring out the set of religions that shaped your own religion, translates to a similar problem other people have, who grew up in other religions.
2. Time symmetry: Figuring out the set of religions that shaped your own religion, translated to a similar problem people had, who grew up in these past religions.

### Growing up as a fundamentalist Christian is like being told your "only" parent only had one parent, or no parents

There is a deeper problem behind the lie that the father told to its child, that he was the only parent.

Where did the father come from?

You see, the child who learns about how children are made, might not only start to question whether their trust in its father was unearned,
but also being overwhelmed with sheer curiosity of figuring who the mother is or was.
On top of that, even though its father seemingly has no curiosity like itself about these things,
it is possible for the child to start thinking who its father could have been, not just how the father behaves.

Obviously, this sheer curiosity is something that makes the child feel different from its father.
There is a simple explanation of its father's behavior: The father is mentally ill.
However, just because there is a simple explanation, does not answer the questions that start to appear,
when the child projects its own curiosity onto its father.
It is like, the child has so much curiosity that there is not even possible to contain this curiosity to a single person.
The curiosity flows over, filling gaps in the holes of lacking curiosity on behalf of other people.

The child can see a deeper lie, behind the lie that the father told it:

The feeling of a "pure" religion must come from the idea it came from only one other religion in the past, or no religion at all.

It is like, discovering that the Earth did not come from an earlier proto-planet, or simply brought into existence,
but from two planets that collided with each other.
Before that, the two planets were formed by collisions of smaller planets.
This process repeated, until there is only dust in space that is needed, to explain how Earth came into existence.

Instead of looking for this idea in the bible, one can simply attribute the idea correctly to the process of Science.

The child figures out that, not only did its father likely have two parents,
but that these two parents also had their own parents,
going back in time, until the ancestors looked like part human and part chimpanzee.

Why would you go looking for this idea in the bible?

The thing is, the idea of a "pure" religion, is simply false,
because the very worldview in this supposedly "pure" religion, came from a set of religions.
However, on top of this, what you now believe is not even confined to a set of religions anymore.

There is a set of processes required, to explain what you believe.

Again, the child is filled with curiosity, about what these processes might be.

### Growing up as a fundamentalist Christian is like being taught there is no way to reason at meta-level about where these beliefs came from

The child keeps expanding how it defines where its beliefs came from.
It moves from one religion, to set of religions, to set of processes.

Not only does the child do this for itself.
It also starts to project these ideas onto people who live in the present, the past and the future.

Next, the child also start to think about these ideas on behalf of other beings in general.
What kind of processes makes a bird behave the way it does?
What about a fish?
What about other planets?
Other galaxies?
Other pocket-universes with different physical laws than in this one?

All these ideas, when attributed correctly, they came from somewhere else.

Finally, you start seeing where the idea came from, that Jesus Christ is God.
This idea does not start with Jesus Christ.

It requires two components:

1. A low literacy, low information environment of some society.
2. The society transitioning into a high literacy, high information environment.

During this transition, people misattributed ideas to their own savior figure,
while they expanded their definitions of sets of processes into a more accurate explanatory picture of the world.

This is the reason why Jesus Christ is seen as God in fundamentalist Christianity.
It is not simply due to low literacy, or ignorance, but the very transition into a high literacy, is causing this feeling,
that there ought to be a "pure" religion in some sense.

The notion of a "pure" religion, is based on a feeling, but this feeling is like a protective shield in a society that undergoes change.
There is no evidence that a such feeling is needed, when growing up in a society that does not change much over time.

From starting with that this feeling of a "pure" religion is important, to realizing that it is not important,
removes the need to explain Jesus Christ as a God, because one understands why people feel the need to do this.

The feeling of a "pure" religion, came from a set of processes.

From an individualistic point of view, one can argue that my personal feeling of a "pure" religion,
when I was growing up, came from texts written in the past about Jesus Christ.
However, due to the space and time symmetry, this idea did not originate with Jesus Christ.
It has always been there, as long religion existed.

I told you, that this blog post was not about me.

The thing is, when people focus on their individual beliefs,
they often miss the bigger picture,
that sets of processes that shape beliefs in general,
does not apply to just a single person.

Two things can be true at the same time about this blog post:

1. This is not about me.
2. This is one way to think about how I became an atheist, after growing up as a fundamentalist Christian.

The actual history of how I became an atheist, is more complex and irrelevant for this blog post.

My point is this:

### Religious misattribution can be avoided when you focus on correct attribution of ideas

Religious misattribution causes a lot of problems.

However, these problems, that religious misattribution causes, are not that significant in the big picture.

When you learn, what you consider important problems, also changes along with the learning process.

The more you learn about where ideas come from, the more you also realize that people often misattribute ideas all over the place.

We can avoid many of the problems that are caused by this habit, simply by focusing more on attributing ideas correctly.

The solution is much simpler, than relating to all sorts of complex historical baggage out there.

This does not imply that correct attribution of ideas is easy to do.
The solution might be simple in some sense, but also hard to achieve in another sense.
